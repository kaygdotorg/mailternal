# Google Gmail OAuth restricted scopes and open-source mail clients

Research checked 2026-09-02; sources are dated/currently maintained 2024–2026 where available. `Official` means Google/Google project documentation; `Project` means the client’s own source/docs; `Developer report` means a third-party account or discussion.

## Bottom line

- `https://mail.google.com/`, `gmail.readonly`, and `gmail.modify` are all **restricted** Gmail scopes; Google’s current scope table labels them restricted, and `mail.google.com` is the IMAP/POP/SMTP full-mail scope. [Official: Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes) · [Official: Gmail XOAUTH2](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/imap/xoauth2-protocol)
- Production use of restricted scopes requires Google OAuth verification plus brand/privacy/domain, scope-minimization, limited-use, and demo-video requirements; restricted scopes add a security assessment **when the app stores or transmits restricted-scope data**, and Google’s verification guide describes that assessment as annual. [Official: verification requirements](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321?hl=en) · [Official: security assessment](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13465431?hl=en)
- “CASA Tier 2” is not universally mandatory merely because a client requests a restricted scope: the required assurance is risk-calculated; Google’s current CASA page uses AL1/AL2, while the FAQ still uses “Tier 2,” and says the tier depends on data sensitivity, user count, and internal risk indicators. [Official: security FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en) · [Official: App Defense Alliance certification](https://www.appdefensealliance.org/certification)
- For a server-backed app, an assessment is the normal restricted-scope path; a genuinely local-only mail client has a materially different posture because Google’s Gmail documentation conditions the assessment on storing/transmitting restricted data on servers. [Official: Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes) · [Project: Mozilla Bugzilla discussion of local-only Thunderbird](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1525483)
- The assessment is annual: Google says restricted-scope apps undergo an annual assessment, all applications are revalidated every year, and certification is valid up to 365 days. [Official: security assessment](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13465431?hl=en) · [Official: ADA certification](https://www.appdefensealliance.org/certification)
- Google does not publish a single universal CASA price; the current public vendor prices I found are TAC’s AASA self-serve starting at **$540** and TAC’s CASA-client ESOF Advanced at **$900 per application/year**; treat these as vendor prices, not a Google fee or guaranteed quote. [Vendor: TAC](https://tacsecurity.com/) · [Vendor: TAC ESOF plans](https://tacsecurity.com/esof-appsec-plans/)
- Free/self-assessment is not a dependable current substitute for a restricted-scope production assessment: Google’s FAQ still refers to a “free tier 2 assessment,” but says that if you ask an authorized assessor to do it, you pay the assessor; a May-2024 Google developer-community post reports Google had removed Tier-2 self-scan as an option for restricted APIs. [Official: Google FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en) · [Developer community report, 2024](https://groups.google.com/g/google-apps-script-community/c/XgsHT2Fr7u8) · [Official: ADA assurance levels](https://www.appdefensealliance.org/certification)
- Fluid Attacks publicly offers a 21-day free trial of its Essential AppSec plan, but that is an AppSec product trial—not evidence that Google will accept it as a free CASA assessment or Letter of Validation. [Vendor: Fluid Attacks](https://docs.fluidattacks.com/quick-start/basics/plans-free-trial)
- The unverified-app cap is **100 new users total over the project’s lifetime**, not 100 concurrently; it cannot be reset or changed, and applies while the unverified screen is shown/testing is used. [Official: unverified apps](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865?hl=en) · [Official: app audience/user cap](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en)
- Personal-use exemption: Google says an app for personal use with fewer than 100 users may continue through the warning screen, but must verify to grow beyond 100; this is not a general “under-100 production apps are exempt” rule. [Official: when verification is not needed](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323?hl=en)
- Internal Workspace/Cloud Identity exemption: an organization-owned project configured as internal and used only by people in that organization is not subject to the unverified screen or 100-user cap, although admin approval may be involved. [Official: internal apps](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323?hl=en)
- Development/testing/staging exemption: Google says verification is not mandatory for those environments, but the unverified screen and 100-user cap still apply there; use a separate production project for public launch. [Official: development/testing](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323?hl=en)
- “Bring your own OAuth client” is a practical distribution pattern, not a Google verification exemption: each developer/user registers and operates a separate client/project, so each client inherits its own consent-screen, cap, verification, and restricted-scope obligations; Google’s IMAP docs still require public apps using user-data scopes to complete verification. [Official: Gmail XOAUTH2](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/imap/xoauth2-protocol) · [Project: NeoMutt](https://neomutt.org/guide/optionalfeatures) · [Project: mutt OAuth README](https://github.com/muttmua/mutt/blob/master/contrib/mutt_oauth2.py.README)

## What open-source mail clients do

- Thunderbird desktop/Mozilla: Mozilla submitted its Gmail OAuth client for verification and reported approval; the project discussion records that local-only storage was treated as not requiring the full security assessment, while still requiring Google review/verification. [Project: Mozilla Bugzilla](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1525483)
- Thunderbird for Android / K-9 Mail: the shared open-source project ships separate OAuth configurations for the Thunderbird and K-9 release apps, and explicitly tells forks to replace the OAuth client setup and use a different redirect URI; this is a maintained, publisher-owned-client model, not BYO for ordinary users. [Project: Thunderbird Android repository](https://github.com/thunderbird/thunderbird-android)
- FairEmail: the maintainer says Google approved OAuth only for the Play Store and GitHub releases, not third-party/F-Droid builds; its current FAQ documents custom OAuth for users who import their own OAuth configuration, and preserves the historical complaint that a yearly $15k–$75k audit was unaffordable (struck-through historical text, not a current official price). [Project: FairEmail FAQ](https://github.com/m66b/FairEmail/blob/master/FAQ.md)
- Geary/GNOME: current Geary guidance routes Gmail sign-in through GNOME Online Accounts, which supplies OAuth2/SSO; users generally do not create or manage a Geary OAuth client. [Project: GNOME Geary FAQ](https://wiki.gnome.org/Apps/Geary/FAQ) · [Project: GNOME account setup](https://teams.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/Websites/help.gnome.org/gnome-help/accounts-add.html)
- aerc: the implementation supports `imaps+oauthbearer` and OAuth token exchange, but the documented configuration requires the user’s own Google client credentials and refresh token; it therefore shifts client registration/verification responsibility to each user. [Project mirror/source history](https://git.benburwell.com/mirrors/aerc/log/lib/oauthbearer.go?h=mbox&showmsg=1) · [Official: Gmail XOAUTH2](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/imap/xoauth2-protocol)
- NeoMutt: its official guide says Gmail OAuth is provided through external scripts and requires the user to obtain their own OAuth client credentials; the companion `mutt_oauth2.py` flow stores the token locally and requests the full `mail.google.com` scope. [Project: NeoMutt guide](https://neomutt.org/guide/optionalfeatures) · [Project: NeoMutt OAuth script](https://github.com/neomutt/neomutt/blob/main/contrib/oauth2/mutt_oauth2.py)
- mutt users: the maintained `mutt_oauth2.py` README likewise tells users to create or obtain a client ID/secret, with “Internal” suggested for an organizational Workspace account and “External” otherwise; each user’s client is consequently their own Google project and compliance surface. [Project: mutt OAuth README](https://github.com/muttmua/mutt/blob/master/contrib/mutt_oauth2.py.README)

## Interpretation for a local-first client

- A local-first client can avoid server-side CASA exposure only if the architecture truly keeps Gmail data/tokens on the user device and the submitted OAuth project still passes Google’s ordinary app/brand verification; this is an interpretation of Google’s “store restricted data on servers” wording plus the Mozilla precedent, not a blanket exemption Google promises for every desktop/mobile app. [Official: Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes) · [Project: Mozilla Bugzilla](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1525483)
- If the client uses IMAP/SMTP, `https://mail.google.com/` is the relevant broad restricted scope; if it can use Gmail API granular scopes, Google explicitly recommends migrating away from the full scope where possible, but `gmail.readonly` and `gmail.modify` remain restricted and are not thereby made verification-free. [Official: Gmail XOAUTH2](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/imap/xoauth2-protocol) · [Official: Gmail scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes)
